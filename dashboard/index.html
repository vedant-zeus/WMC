Round Robin (RR)

Baseline 1
Metric: -t_last_served
Allocates PRBs sequentially to users in cyclic order, completely ignoring instantaneous channel quality (CQI). Maximizes fairness at the cost of poor spectral efficiency and severe video packet loss under load.

Max C/I (Carrier-to-Interference)

Baseline 2
Metric: r_i,k (instant rate)
Pure greedy opportunistic scheduler: gives every PRB to the user with the highest achievable rate. Delivers maximum aggregate cell throughput under high load, but starves cell-edge users with lower SINR (catastrophic fairness breakdown).

Proportional Fair (PF)

Baseline 3 (Standard)
Metric: r_i,k / R_bar_i
Industry standard for 4G LTE. Balances instantaneous peak rate against past average served rate. Does not consider packet deadlines or traffic QoS classes, leading to packet drops for VoIP and video near capacity.

M-LWDF (Delay-Aware)

Baseline 4 (QoS)
Metric: a_i · D_HOL,i · r_i,k / R_bar_i
Modified Largest Weighted Delay First. Weights the PF metric linearly by head-of-line delay. Improves real-time packet delivery, but linear delay weights can cause wild swings in video latency and high BLER under channel mismatches.

PULSE (Proposed)

Integrated C-RAN
Metric: w_class · U(t) · pref_sfr · r / R_bar
Integrated cross-layer management: (1) Bounded exponential priority-urgency weighting, (2) Centralized C-RAN Interference Coordination (ICA), (3) Outer-Loop Link Adaptation (OLLA), (4) Load-adaptive SFR, and (5) Slack-aware DTX sleep.

    Wireless & Mobile Communications (WMC) Theory Companion

    Theoretical background, mathematical formulations and evaluation guide for oral examination

    1. LTE Physical Layer & PRB Structure

    LTE operates with a 10 ms Radio Frame divided into ten 1 ms Subframes (TTIs). Each 1 ms TTI comprises two 0.5 ms slots. In the frequency domain, resources are partitioned into subcarriers spaced at 15 kHz.

    The fundamental unit of radio resource assignment is the Physical Resource Block (PRB): 12 subcarriers (180 kHz) × 7 OFDM symbols (0.5 ms slot) = 84 Resource Elements (REs). In a 1 ms TTI pair, there are 168 REs, of which ~120 REs carry user data after control channel (PDCCH) and cell-specific reference signals (CRS) overhead.

    5 MHz BW = 25 PRBs 1 TTI = 1 ms Subcarrier: 15 kHz 120 Data REs / PRB

    2. eNodeB Architecture & Energy Model

    The eNodeB (Evolved Node B) is the base station in LTE, combining radio transceiver equipment with the MAC packet scheduler. Modern telecom power consumption is dominated by the base station power amplifier (PA) and baseband processing.

    We implement the standardized EARTH project energy model for single-carrier macro cells:
    P_cell = P_0 + Δ_p · P_max · (used_PRBs / N_RB) when active (P_0 = 130 W, Δ_p = 4.7, P_max = 20 W), and P_sleep = 75 W when the cell enters micro-sleep Discontinuous Transmission (DTX).

    Active P0: 130 W Micro-sleep: 75 W 42.3% Power Saving

    3. CQI Feedback & Adaptive Modulation (AMC)

    User Equipment (UE) periodically reports a 4-bit Channel Quality Indicator (CQI 1–15) reflecting downlink SINR based on 3GPP TS 36.213 Table 7.2.3-1. The eNodeB chooses the Modulation and Coding Scheme (MCS):

    • CQI 1–6: QPSK (Spectral Efficiency 0.15 to 0.88 bits/RE)
    • CQI 7–9: 16QAM (Spectral Efficiency 1.18 to 1.91 bits/RE)
    • CQI 10–15: 64QAM (Spectral Efficiency 2.41 to 5.55 bits/RE)

    Target BLER: 10% HARQ RTT: 8 ms CQI Delay: 4 ms

    4. The Flashlight Effect & Centralized ICA

    Because CQI feedback is delayed by 4 ms, the interference environment measured by the UE is often obsolete by the time the eNodeB transmits. If an interfering neighbor cell was idle at measurement time but transmits now, the UE suffers unexpected packet drops (NACKs).

    PULSE solves this using Interference-Coordinated Link Adaptation (ICA). Operating as a centralized C-RAN controller with full awareness of neighbor PRB allocations, it adjusts reported SINR:
    SINR_corrected = SINR_rep · (N0 + I_then) / (N0 + I_now)

    Flashlight Correction OLLA Offset Zero Packet Loss Jumps

    5. Traffic Classes & Delay Budgets (QCI)

    LTE defines QoS Class Identifiers (QCI) with strict latency and packet loss thresholds:

    • VoIP (QCI 1): AMR 12.2 kbps codec, 328-bit frame every 20 ms. Strict delay budget: 100 ms.
    • HD Video (QCI 2): 450 kbps stream, 40 ms frame interval (25 fps). Delay budget: 300 ms.
    • Best Effort (QCI 9): Web downloads / file transfers with elastic exponential file arrival.

    VoIP: 100 ms budget Video: 300 ms budget BE: Elastic

    6. Fairness & Performance Metrics

    Jain's Fairness Index: J = (Σ sat_i)² / (N · Σ sat_i²), where satisfaction is the ratio of served bits to offered bits. J = 1.0 represents perfect equality.

    Cell-Edge Satisfaction: The 5th percentile of user throughput, ensuring that vulnerable users located at the boundary of adjacent cells are not starved of service.
